Every January, 1099 forms start arriving — from clients, banks, investment platforms, and the IRS itself. If you're a freelancer, contractor, or investor, these forms can be confusing. Here's everything you need to know.

## What Is a 1099?

A 1099 is an **information return** — a form sent to both you and the IRS reporting income you received that wasn't reported on a W-2. It's the government's way of tracking non-employment income.

You'll receive a 1099 from anyone who paid you $600 or more during the year (for services), or from financial institutions reporting interest, dividends, and transactions.

## The Most Common 1099 Types

### 1099-NEC (Non-Employee Compensation)
**Who sends it**: Clients and businesses that paid you $600+ for freelance work, contracting, or services.
**Box 1**: Your total payment — this is self-employment income, subject to both income tax and self-employment tax (15.3%).
**Action**: Report on Schedule C of your tax return.

### 1099-MISC (Miscellaneous Income)
**Who sends it**: Businesses for rent, royalties, prizes, legal settlements, and other payments.
**Key boxes**:
- Box 1: Rents
- Box 2: Royalties
- Box 3: Other income (prizes, awards)
- Box 6: Medical/health care payments
**Action**: Varies by box — rent goes to Schedule E, other income to Schedule 1.

### 1099-INT (Interest Income)
**Who sends it**: Banks and credit unions when you earn $10+ in interest.
**Box 1**: Total interest earned.
**Box 4**: Federal income tax withheld (if any).
**Action**: Report on Schedule B. If total interest across all accounts is under $1,500, you can report it directly on Form 1040.

### 1099-DIV (Dividend Income)
**Who sends it**: Brokerage accounts and mutual funds.
**Key boxes**:
- Box 1a: Total ordinary dividends
- Box 1b: Qualified dividends (taxed at lower capital gains rates)
- Box 2a: Total capital gain distributions
**Action**: Report on Schedule B and Schedule D as applicable.

### 1099-B (Proceeds from Broker Transactions)
**Who sends it**: Your brokerage when you sell stocks, bonds, or crypto (on some platforms).
**Key information**:
- Date acquired and date sold
- Cost basis (what you paid)
- Proceeds (what you received)
- Whether the gain/loss is short-term or long-term
**Action**: Report each transaction on Form 8949, then summarize on Schedule D.

### 1099-R (Retirement Distributions)
**Who sends it**: Pension plans, IRAs, 401(k)s when you take a distribution.
**Box 1**: Gross distribution
**Box 2a**: Taxable amount
**Box 7**: Distribution code — critical for determining taxes and whether an early withdrawal penalty applies
**Action**: Report on Form 1040. If Box 7 has code 1 and you're under 59½, you'll also owe a 10% penalty.

### 1099-G (Government Payments)
**Who sends it**: State or federal government.
**Common uses**: Unemployment compensation (Box 1), state tax refunds (Box 2).
**Important**: Unemployment income is fully taxable. Report on Schedule 1.

### 1099-K (Payment Card and Third-Party Network Transactions)
**Who sends it**: Payment processors like PayPal, Venmo (business accounts), Stripe, Square, Etsy.
**What it covers**: Business payments processed through these platforms.
**Current threshold (2024+)**: $5,000+ (IRS revised this threshold multiple times; check the current year's rules).
**Action**: Report as business income on Schedule C. Keep records to offset with business expenses.

## Reading Your 1099: Box by Box

Every 1099 has:
- **Payer information** (top left): Who sent you money
- **Recipient information**: Your name, address, and TIN (SSN or EIN)
- **Account number**: Reference for that payer's records
- **Numbered boxes**: The actual income and withholding amounts

## What to Do If Your 1099 Is Wrong

If the amount on a 1099 is incorrect:

1. **Contact the payer** immediately and ask for a corrected 1099 (Form 1099 with the "CORRECTED" box checked)
2. **Don't wait** — corrected forms should be issued by mid-February; you'll need them before April 15
3. **If you can't get it corrected in time**: Report the correct amount on your tax return with a notation explaining the discrepancy

**Never simply omit a 1099 you disagree with.** The IRS received a copy of the same form. Discrepancies trigger automated notices (like the CP2000).

## What If You Didn't Receive a 1099 You Expected?

You must report all income — even without a 1099. If a client paid you $800 and didn't send a 1099, that income is still taxable. Report it on Schedule C.

If you're waiting for a 1099 that hasn't arrived:
- Check with the payer — it may have been sent to an old address
- Log into your accounts — many platforms post 1099s digitally in January
- Request a copy from the payer
