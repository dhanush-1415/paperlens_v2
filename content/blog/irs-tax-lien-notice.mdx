If you've received a Notice of Federal Tax Lien (NFTL) or discovered one filed against you, the IRS has staked a legal claim against your property. This is not the same as a levy — nothing has been seized yet. But a federal tax lien is serious, public, and affects your ability to sell property, borrow money, and maintain your credit. Here's what it actually means and what you can do about it.

## What Is a Federal Tax Lien?

A federal tax lien is the IRS's **legal claim against all of your current and future property** to secure payment of a tax debt. Once a lien exists, the IRS has an interest in:
- Your real estate (home, land, investment property)
- Personal property (vehicles, boats, jewelry, equipment)
- Financial assets (bank accounts, investment accounts, business assets)
- Future assets you acquire while the lien is active

The lien doesn't mean the IRS has taken anything. It means they have a **legal claim** that must be satisfied before you can freely transfer those assets to someone else.

## How a Federal Tax Lien Is Created

A lien arises automatically when:
1. The IRS assesses a tax liability (determines you owe money)
2. They send you a bill (usually a CP14 or subsequent notice)
3. You fail to pay in full within 10 days of the demand

The lien attaches to your property at the moment of assessment — but it only becomes enforceable against third parties (banks, other creditors, buyers) when the IRS files the **Notice of Federal Tax Lien** in the public records of the county where you live or own property.

## The Notice of Federal Tax Lien Filing

The NFTL is filed with the county recorder or clerk's office. This makes the lien a matter of public record. Anyone who searches your name in property records will see it — including lenders, title companies, and future employers who do background checks.

Before filing, the IRS is required to give you a **Collection Due Process (CDP) hearing notice** (usually a Letter 1058 or CP90). This is your formal notification that the IRS intends to take enforced collection action. You have **30 days** from this notice to request a CDP hearing and challenge the lien filing. This is one of the most important windows in the entire IRS collection process — do not miss it.

## How a Federal Tax Lien Affects Your Life

### Credit Reports
The IRS stopped reporting tax liens to the major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) in 2018 for personal tax liabilities. Most personal federal tax liens no longer appear on consumer credit reports. Business tax liens and liens in certain states may still appear. However, any lender doing a title search before issuing a mortgage or refinancing will find the lien in county records regardless.

### Selling Property
When you sell real estate with a federal tax lien attached, the lien must be paid at or before closing. The title company will discover it in the title search. You cannot transfer clear title to a buyer until the lien is resolved. The IRS can receive payment from the sale proceeds directly.

### Refinancing
Most mortgage lenders will not refinance a property with a federal tax lien without a subordination agreement (discussed below). The lien makes the lender's security interest junior to the IRS's claim, which they won't accept.

### Starting a Business
If you try to open a business bank account or seek business credit, the lien may surface in checks run by the financial institution.

## The 4 Ways to Get a Federal Tax Lien Released

### 1. Pay the Tax Debt in Full

The most direct resolution. Once you pay the full amount owed — taxes, penalties, and interest — the IRS must release the lien within **30 days** of payment. Request a formal "Certificate of Release" and record it in the same county where the NFTL was filed. Confirm the release has been recorded before attempting any real estate transactions.

### 2. Lien Discharge

A discharge removes the lien from a **specific piece of property** — useful when you want to sell one property to raise funds. The lien is released from the property you're selling, and the IRS is paid from the proceeds. The lien continues on your other property.

To apply, file **Form 14135 (Application for Certificate of Discharge)** with the IRS. This requires proof of value and a plan for how the IRS will be paid from the transaction.

### 3. Lien Subordination

Subordination doesn't remove the lien — it moves the IRS to a **secondary position** behind another creditor (typically a mortgage lender). This is used to enable refinancing when a lender won't accept a junior position behind the IRS.

Apply with **Form 14134 (Application for Certificate of Subordination)**. The IRS will agree to subordinate if the transaction ultimately results in better collection for them (for example, cash-out refinance proceeds go to pay the IRS, or the refinance lowers your payment and makes it more likely you'll pay the tax debt).

### 4. Lien Withdrawal

A withdrawal removes the NFTL from public record entirely — as if it was never filed. This is more powerful than a release (which shows the debt was satisfied) because a withdrawal erases the public record.

The IRS can withdraw a lien under the Fresh Start program if:
- The tax debt has been paid in full or becomes legally unenforceable
- You have entered into a direct debit installment agreement (DDIA) for the full amount owed and have made at least 3 consecutive timely payments
- You owe $25,000 or less (qualifying installment agreement)
- The withdrawal is in the best interest of the taxpayer and the government

Request a withdrawal using **Form 12277 (Application for Withdrawal of Filed Form 668(Y) Notice of Federal Tax Lien)**.

## Lien vs. Levy: The Key Difference

Confusion between these two terms is common:

| | **Lien** | **Levy** |
|---|----------|----------|
| What it is | Legal claim on property | Actual seizure of property |
| Effect | Clouds title, blocks transfers | Takes your money or assets |
| Notice required | CDP notice before filing | CDP notice + 30-day waiting period |
| Action required | Respond to CDP, pay, or arrange relief | Call IRS immediately or assets are gone |

A lien is a warning and a claim. A levy is enforcement. The IRS typically files a lien before escalating to a levy.

## The IRS Fresh Start Program

The IRS Fresh Start program expanded relief options for taxpayers with tax liens:
- Lien withdrawal available for qualifying installment agreements on balances under $25,000
- Easier access to installment agreements (up to $50,000 with a streamlined process)
- More flexibility in qualifying for Offer in Compromise

If you have a tax lien and want to resolve it without paying in full immediately, Fresh Start may be your path. Work with an enrolled agent or tax attorney to evaluate your qualification.

## What to Do Right Now

1. Confirm the balance owed — request an Account Transcript from IRS.gov
2. If you received a CDP notice, decide whether to request a hearing (30-day deadline)
3. Evaluate whether you can pay in full, need a payment plan, or qualify for OIC
4. Contact the lender or title company if a real estate transaction is pending — they need to know and can help coordinate IRS payoff
