Student loan servicers send a lot of mail. Most people read the subject line, feel a vague sense of concern, and set the letter aside. That's a mistake — because different notices represent dramatically different situations, and missing the right response window can send you from delinquency to default to wage garnishment faster than you'd expect.

Here is what every type of student loan notice means and what you need to do.

## Grace Period Ending Notice

When you graduate, leave school, or drop below half-time enrollment, federal student loans enter a **grace period** — typically 6 months for Direct Loans and unsubsidized Stafford Loans (3 years for Perkins Loans).

A grace period ending notice means your first payment is approaching. What you need to do:
- Confirm your repayment plan (default is the Standard 10-year plan)
- If the standard payment is unaffordable, apply for an Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) plan now — before the grace period ends
- Update your contact information with your servicer
- Set up autopay — most servicers offer a 0.25% rate reduction

If you miss your first payment because you didn't act on the grace period notice, you're already delinquent on day one.

## Repayment Plan Change Notice

Your servicer is telling you your repayment plan has changed — either because you requested it, you defaulted on a previous plan, or federal policy changed your plan. Read this notice carefully:

- What is your new monthly payment amount?
- What is your new interest rate (if applicable)?
- How does this affect your total repayment timeline?
- If you're on an IDR plan, has your monthly payment changed due to recertification?

If you didn't request the change and don't understand why it happened, call your servicer immediately. Errors in plan administration do occur.

## IDR Recertification Notice

Income-Driven Repayment plans (SAVE, IBR, PAYE, ICR) require **annual recertification**. You must submit updated income and family size information every year. Your servicer will send a recertification notice approximately 60–90 days before your deadline.

What happens if you miss recertification:
- Your payment reverts to the amount required under the Standard 10-year plan
- Unpaid interest that was previously waived under SAVE may capitalize
- You remain enrolled in IDR but at a much higher payment

File your recertification immediately when you receive the notice. It takes about 10 minutes at StudentAid.gov.

## Delinquency Notice

You are **delinquent** starting the day after a missed payment. Servicers send delinquency notices at various intervals:

- **1–29 days delinquent**: First contact, usually a reminder
- **30–89 days delinquent**: More urgent notice, credit bureau reporting begins at 90 days
- **90 days delinquent**: Your servicer officially reports the delinquency to all three major credit bureaus, which can reduce your credit score significantly

What to do immediately:
1. Call your servicer before the 90-day mark
2. Ask about forbearance or deferment if you're experiencing temporary hardship
3. Apply for an IDR plan if your payment is unaffordable
4. Make at least a partial payment to show good faith and buy time

Delinquency is recoverable. Default is far harder to undo.

## Default Notice: The 270-Day Line

Federal student loans enter **default** after **270 days of non-payment** (approximately 9 months). Some loan types default faster: FFEL Loans can default after 270 days, and Perkins Loans can default after the institution's designated period.

When you receive a default notice, or when your loan enters default status, the consequences are severe and immediate:

- The **entire loan balance** becomes due immediately (acceleration)
- Collection fees of up to **25% of the principal and interest** can be added
- Your credit report shows "default" — serious damage to your credit score
- Your federal tax refund can be seized (**Treasury Offset Program**)
- Your wages can be garnished (up to 15% of disposable pay) without a court judgment
- You lose eligibility for additional federal student aid
- You lose access to IDR plans and deferment until you exit default

## Wage Garnishment Notice (Administrative Wage Garnishment)

Unlike most debt collectors, the federal government does not need to sue you in court before garnishing your wages for defaulted federal student loans. The Department of Education uses **Administrative Wage Garnishment (AWG)** — a direct notice to your employer to withhold up to 15% of your disposable pay.

You should receive a notice at least **30 days before** garnishment begins. During that 30-day window you can:
- Request a hearing to challenge the garnishment
- Resolve the default through rehabilitation or consolidation (see below)
- Make an agreement to pay voluntarily

Act immediately on any AWG notice. Once garnishment begins, stopping it requires either resolving the default or winning a hearing.

## Treasury Offset Notice

The **Treasury Offset Program (TOP)** allows the government to intercept:
- Federal tax refunds
- Social Security benefits (in some cases)
- Federal payments

You should receive a notice before the offset occurs. Once your refund has been seized, you can dispute it if you believe the offset was in error — but recovering seized funds is difficult.

## Exiting Default: Rehabilitation vs. Consolidation

### Loan Rehabilitation
You agree to make **9 voluntary, reasonable, and affordable monthly payments** in a 10-month period (payments can be as low as $5 for extremely low incomes). After successful rehabilitation:
- The default notation is removed from your credit report
- Collection fees may be waived or reduced
- You regain access to IDR plans, deferment, and forbearance
- You can only rehabilitate each loan once

### Direct Consolidation
You consolidate your defaulted loans into a new Direct Consolidation Loan. Faster than rehabilitation (can be completed in weeks vs. months), but:
- The default notation remains on your credit report (though it's marked "paid")
- You must agree to repay under an IDR plan
- You must make 3 consecutive payments before the consolidation is processed

Choose rehabilitation if credit report impact is your priority. Choose consolidation if speed is your priority.

## Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) Notices

PSLF forgives the remaining balance of Direct Loans after 120 qualifying payments while working full-time for a qualifying employer (government, 501(c)(3) nonprofit, or certain other public service organizations).

Key notices to watch for:
- **Annual Employment Certification confirmation**: Your employer and payment count have been verified
- **PSLF eligibility notice**: Confirmation you're on track
- **Denial notice**: Your employment or payment count didn't qualify — you have the right to appeal

If you receive a PSLF denial, review the specific reason carefully. Denials for payment count issues or ineligible repayment plans can sometimes be corrected retroactively.
