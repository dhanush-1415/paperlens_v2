You filed your taxes. You thought you were done. Then a CP11 arrives saying you owe money you didn't expect. Here's what happened and what to do about it.

## What Is a CP11?

The CP11 is a notice the IRS sends when it has **made changes to your tax return** and those changes result in a balance due. Unlike the CP2000 (which is a proposed change), the CP11 represents a **correction already applied** — the IRS has already updated your account.

Common reasons the IRS sends a CP11:
- A math error on your original return
- An incorrectly claimed credit (Child Tax Credit, Earned Income Credit, etc.)
- A discrepancy between your return and forms filed by employers or payers
- A deduction the IRS disallowed (often education credits or dependent claims)

## Reading the CP11

The CP11 shows you:

1. **The change** — what line item was adjusted and by how much
2. **The original amount** you claimed
3. **The corrected amount** the IRS is using
4. **The new balance due** — including any interest from the original due date
5. **The payment deadline** — typically 21 days from the notice date
6. **Instructions to dispute** — if you disagree

The comparison table in the middle of the notice is the most important part. Read it carefully.

## When the IRS Is Right

If you review the change and the IRS is correct — a math error, a credit you miscalculated — the simplest path is to pay the amount due by the deadline. You can pay online at IRS.gov/payments.

If you can't pay in full, request an installment agreement at IRS.gov/opa before the deadline. Interest continues to accrue, but penalties for failure to pay are smaller when you're on a plan.

## When the IRS Is Wrong

The IRS makes mistakes. If you believe the CP11 is incorrect:

**Step 1: Gather your documentation**
- Your original return
- W-2s, 1099s, and any supporting forms for the item in question
- Receipts if a deduction is being disallowed

**Step 2: Write a response letter**
- Reference the CP11 notice number and your tax year
- Clearly state what you believe is incorrect and why
- Include copies (not originals) of your supporting documents

**Step 3: Mail it to the address on the notice**
Send by certified mail with return receipt. Keep copies of everything.

**Step 4: Follow up**
The IRS takes 30–60 days to process written responses. If you don't hear back, call the number on the notice.

## Common Errors the IRS Makes on CP11s

- **Earned Income Credit (EITC)**: Disallowed due to income calculations or dependent eligibility — often worth disputing with proper documentation
- **Education credits**: The American Opportunity Credit has strict eligibility; IRS sometimes flags it incorrectly
- **Child and Dependent Care Credit**: Disallowed when provider TINs don't match
- **Premium Tax Credit**: Reconciliation errors when marketplace coverage was involved

## What Happens If You Ignore a CP11

Unlike a CP2000 (which is proposed), the CP11 reflects a change already made to your account. Ignoring it means:
- Interest accrues daily on the unpaid balance
- Failure-to-pay penalty adds 0.5% per month
- The IRS will send follow-up notices (CP501, CP503) and eventually levy notices

## Your Rights

You have the right to:
- **Disagree and respond** in writing within 60 days of the notice date
- **Request an audit reconsideration** if the change was made without examining your original return
- **Petition Tax Court** if you receive a Statutory Notice of Deficiency (a separate notice the IRS must send before assessing certain taxes)
