The CP90 is not a reminder. It is the **Final Notice of Intent to Levy and Notice of Your Right to a Hearing**. If you have this letter, enforcement is imminent. You have a legally defined window to act — and once it closes, your options become far more limited.

## What Is a CP90?

The CP90 (also called LT11 in some versions) is the IRS's formal declaration that it intends to levy your property. It is issued after all prior notices — CP14, CP501, CP503, CP504 — have gone unanswered.

This notice:

- Is sent by **certified mail** (the IRS has legal proof of delivery)
- Triggers a **30-day response window**
- Grants you the right to request a **Collection Due Process (CDP) hearing**
- Authorizes the IRS to seize wages, bank accounts, Social Security benefits, and property after the window closes

## What Can the IRS Levy After a CP90?

- **Wages**: A continuous wage levy that takes a portion of every paycheck
- **Bank accounts**: A one-time freeze and seizure of all available funds (with a 21-day hold period)
- **Social Security payments**: Up to 15% withheld
- **Retirement accounts**: IRAs and 401(k)s can be levied (taxes + 10% penalty for early withdrawal apply)
- **Business assets**: Accounts receivable, equipment
- **Real property**: With additional steps, even your home

## Your Most Important Option: The CDP Hearing

The single most valuable right you have right now is the **Collection Due Process hearing**. Requesting it does two things:

1. **Legally pauses all collection activity** while your case is under review
2. **Preserves your right to appeal** the IRS's decision to Tax Court

To request a CDP hearing, complete **Form 12153 (Request for a Collection Due Process or Equivalent Hearing)** and mail it to the address on your notice — postmarked within 30 days of the notice date.

At the CDP hearing, you can:

- Challenge whether you actually owe the debt
- Propose alternative collection methods (installment agreement, OIC)
- Claim financial hardship
- Argue that the levy is inappropriate given your circumstances

> **Critical**: If you miss the 30-day CDP deadline, you can still request an "Equivalent Hearing" — but you lose the right to appeal to Tax Court.

## Other Options You Have Right Now

### Pay in Full

If you can pay the balance, do it now at IRS.gov/payments. The levy threat ends immediately.

### Installment Agreement

If you owe $50,000 or less, you can often get a payment plan approved online that will stop wage and bank levies. A plan won't stop a levy already in progress.

### Offer in Compromise

If you can't pay the full balance, an OIC lets you settle for less. The application must be submitted before a levy is served to get the full benefit of the application hold.

### Penalty Abatement

If the penalties are a significant part of what you owe and you have a history of compliance, you may qualify for first-time penalty abatement — which can reduce the balance substantially.

### Innocent Spouse Relief

If the debt arises from a joint return but was due to your spouse's actions, you may be able to get relief from the liability.

## Get a Tax Professional

At the CP90 stage, a tax professional — an Enrolled Agent, CPA, or tax attorney — is usually worth the cost. They can:

- File the CDP hearing request correctly and on time
- Negotiate directly with the IRS
- Propose collection alternatives the IRS is more likely to accept
- Stop a levy that has already been served (this requires immediate action)

## Emergency: A Levy Has Already Started

If wages are already being garnished or your bank account is frozen, you still have options:

- **Request an immediate levy release** by calling the IRS and demonstrating economic hardship
- Have a professional file Form 911 (Request for Taxpayer Advocate Service Assistance)
- Contact the Taxpayer Advocate Service at 1-877-777-4778

A levy can be released — but it requires proving hardship and often having a payment arrangement in place.
