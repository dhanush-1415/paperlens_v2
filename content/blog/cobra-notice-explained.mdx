COBRA is simultaneously the most useful and most expensive health insurance option most Americans will ever encounter. When you lose job-based coverage, a COBRA election notice arrives — giving you 60 days to decide whether to continue your existing coverage. Here's what you need to know before that deadline.

## What Is COBRA?

COBRA (Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act) is a federal law that lets employees and their families continue group health insurance coverage for a limited time after certain events — most commonly job loss.

The key distinction: under COBRA, you're keeping the exact same plan you had at work. The quality doesn't change. But you now pay 100% of the premium (both your share and your employer's share) plus up to 2% administrative fee.

## What's in the COBRA Notice?

Your COBRA election notice must include:

1. **The qualifying event** that triggered COBRA eligibility (termination, reduction in hours, divorce from covered employee, etc.)
2. **Your coverage options** — which plans you can elect
3. **The monthly premium** you'd pay for each option
4. **The election deadline** — 60 days from the later of: the date of the event, or the date of the notice
5. **Duration of coverage** available (typically 18 or 36 months)
6. **How to pay** — the administrator's contact information and payment process

## Qualifying Events for COBRA

COBRA applies when group health insurance is lost due to:

- **Voluntary or involuntary termination** (including layoffs, but not gross misconduct)
- **Reduction in hours** that causes loss of benefits eligibility
- **Employee death** (for covered dependents)
- **Divorce or legal separation** from the covered employee
- **A dependent child aging out** of coverage (typically at 26)
- **Employee becoming eligible for Medicare** (for dependents who then lose coverage)

## How Much Does COBRA Cost?

The sticker shock is real. Most employers pay 70–80% of health insurance premiums for employees. COBRA makes you pay all of it.

**Example**:

- Your employer's total plan cost: $700/month per employee
- You were paying: $140/month (20%)
- COBRA cost: $714/month ($700 + 2% admin fee)

This can be $500–$1,500+/month for family coverage.

## Should You Elect COBRA?

It depends on your situation:

### COBRA makes sense when:

- You have ongoing medical needs, existing prescriptions, or scheduled procedures — COBRA is retroactive (you can elect after incurring expenses, within the 60-day window)
- Your current providers are in-network on your current plan but may not be on alternatives
- You expect to get new employer coverage within a few months
- ACA marketplace options in your area have limited networks or high out-of-pocket costs

### Consider alternatives when:

- You're generally healthy and the cost difference is significant
- You qualify for Medicaid (losing job-based coverage is a qualifying life event)
- You have access to a spouse's employer plan (also a qualifying life event)
- ACA marketplace plans in your area are cheaper with similar coverage

## COBRA vs. ACA Marketplace

Losing job-based coverage triggers a Special Enrollment Period (60 days) for ACA marketplace plans. This lets you compare:

- **COBRA**: Same plan, often much more expensive, immediate continuity
- **Marketplace plan**: New plan with income-based subsidies (if you qualify), potentially much cheaper

**Key**: If your projected income for the year will be under 400% of the federal poverty level, marketplace subsidies may make a comparable plan far cheaper than COBRA.

## The 60-Day Retroactive Window

COBRA has a powerful feature most people don't know: if you elect COBRA within 60 days, your coverage is **retroactive to the date of the qualifying event**. This means:

- You can wait to see if you incur major medical expenses
- If you don't, you pay nothing (just don't elect)
- If you do, you can elect COBRA retroactively and your claims will be covered
- You'll owe back premiums for the months of retroactive coverage

This strategy carries risk (you're effectively uninsured in the interim), but for healthy individuals, it can make financial sense.

## What Happens If You Don't Elect COBRA?

You simply lose your employer-based coverage at the end of the month your qualifying event occurred (or sometimes the date itself). You have no COBRA rights after the 60-day election window closes.

## Duration of COBRA Coverage

Standard COBRA lasts **18 months** for job loss or reduction in hours. It can extend to:

- **29 months** if you're disabled at the time of the qualifying event
- **36 months** for qualifying events like divorce, death, or dependent aging out
