Most employees glance at the "net pay" number and move on. But the rest of your pay stub contains information that directly affects your taxes, benefits, and retirement — and errors in it cost you money. Here's how to read every line.

## The Big Picture: Gross vs. Net

**Gross Pay**: What you earned before any deductions. This is your base salary or hourly rate × hours worked, plus any overtime, bonuses, or commissions.

**Net Pay**: What actually gets deposited. Gross pay minus all deductions. The gap between these two numbers is everything this article explains.

## The Main Sections of a Pay Stub

### 1. Earnings

This section shows how your gross pay was calculated:

| Item | What It Means |
|------|--------------|
| Regular | Base pay for standard hours |
| Overtime | Hours over 40/week at 1.5× rate |
| Bonus | Any bonus payment this period |
| Commission | Sales-based earnings |
| Holiday Pay | Pay for company holidays |
| PTO/Vacation | Paid time off used |

Check that your hourly rate and hours are accurate. Overtime miscalculations are a common employer error.

### 2. Federal Tax Withholding

**Federal Income Tax (FIT)**: Your employer withholds this based on your W-4 and your pay. The amount depends on your filing status, pay frequency, and any additional withholding you requested. Your W-4 controls this — if you consistently owe or get a large refund at tax time, adjusting your W-4 fixes it.

**Social Security Tax**: 6.2% of your gross wages, up to the annual wage base ($168,600 in 2024). Your employer matches this.

**Medicare Tax**: 1.45% of all gross wages (no cap). An additional 0.9% applies to wages over $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married). Your employer matches the base 1.45%.

These three together are called **FICA taxes** (Federal Insurance Contributions Act). They total 7.65% of your gross pay up to the Social Security cap.

### 3. State and Local Tax Withholding

**State Income Tax**: Depends on your state. Some states (Texas, Florida, Washington) have no income tax. Others range from 2–13%. Your state W-4 equivalent controls this.

**Local/City Tax**: Some cities (New York City, Philadelphia, San Francisco) have additional local income taxes. Check if your city does.

**State Disability Insurance (SDI)**: States like California, New Jersey, and New York withhold a small percentage for state disability programs.

**State Unemployment Insurance (SUI)**: Typically paid by the employer, not the employee. If you see it on your stub, verify with HR.

### 4. Pre-Tax Deductions

These reduce your taxable income — dollar-for-dollar — before taxes are calculated, making them especially valuable:

- **401(k) / 403(b) contributions**: Your retirement savings. Traditional 401(k) reduces federal and most state taxes now; Roth 401(k) doesn't reduce taxes now but grows tax-free.
- **Health insurance premiums**: What you pay for employer-sponsored health, dental, and vision.
- **HSA contributions**: Health Savings Account deposits (pre-tax, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for medical expenses).
- **FSA contributions**: Flexible Spending Account — use-it-or-lose-it funds for medical or dependent care expenses.
- **Commuter benefits**: Transit or parking contributions.
- **Life insurance premiums**: Employer-sponsored life coverage (first $50,000 is employer-paid; additional coverage may show here).

### 5. Post-Tax Deductions

These come out after taxes are calculated — no tax benefit:

- **Roth 401(k)**: After-tax retirement contributions
- **Charitable payroll deductions**: If you set up automatic donations through your employer
- **Union dues**: If applicable
- **Wage garnishments**: Court-ordered deductions for child support, student loans, or debt

### 6. Employer Contributions (Informational)

Your pay stub may show what your employer contributes on your behalf — these aren't deductions from your pay, just informational:

- **Employer 401(k) match**: What your company adds to your retirement account
- **Employer health insurance**: The share your employer pays (often much larger than your portion)
- **Employer FICA**: Their matching 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare

## Year-to-Date (YTD) Columns

The YTD column is critical. It shows cumulative totals for the year, which helps you:

- **Track Social Security cap**: Once your YTD wages hit the Social Security wage base, that withholding stops
- **Verify annual totals**: Cross-reference with your W-2 at year end
- **Track 401(k) contributions**: Make sure you're on track for your annual goal
- **Spot errors**: If a deduction looks wrong in YTD, investigate

## Common Pay Stub Errors

- **Wrong hourly rate** after a raise
- **Missing overtime** or miscalculated overtime rate
- **Benefits deduction continues** after you changed plans
- **Wrong W-4 reflected** (filing status, extra withholding)
- **401(k) contribution not matching** what you elected
- **Double deduction** for the same benefit
- **HSA deduction not showing up** despite your enrollment

If you find an error, contact payroll in writing. Keep a record.
