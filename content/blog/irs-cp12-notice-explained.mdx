A CP12 is one of the more pleasant IRS notices to receive — though it's still worth reading carefully. Here's exactly what it means and what (if anything) you need to do.

## What Is a CP12?

The CP12 means the IRS **found an error on your tax return and corrected it**, resulting in a **change to your refund**. This could mean:

- Your refund is **larger** than you originally calculated (IRS found a mistake in your favor)
- Your refund is **smaller** than you expected (IRS corrected an overclaim)
- Your expected refund was **eliminated** and you now owe a small balance

The most common scenario is a corrected refund amount due to math errors or incorrectly calculated credits.

## Why Did You Get a CP12?

Common causes:

- **Math error** on your return — a simple addition or subtraction mistake
- **Withholding discrepancy** — what you entered didn't match what your employer reported
- **Credit calculation error** — Child Tax Credit, Earned Income Credit, or similar
- **Incorrect standard deduction** — wrong amount for your filing status
- **Social Security income calculation** — the taxable portion was computed incorrectly

## Reading the CP12

The notice shows you:

1. **Original refund amount** — what you claimed
2. **Corrected refund amount** — what the IRS calculated
3. **What changed** — a table showing which line items were adjusted
4. **When to expect your refund** — if it's larger, typically 4–6 weeks from the notice date
5. **How to dispute** — instructions if you disagree

Look at the comparison table carefully. The IRS will show you the line number, your original amount, and their corrected amount side by side.

## If Your Refund Is Larger

Good news — you don't need to do anything. The IRS will send the corrected (higher) refund to the bank account or address on file. If you see the notice and your refund hasn't arrived in 6 weeks, call the number on the notice.

## If Your Refund Is Smaller

Review the change carefully. If the IRS is right — a math error, for example — accept the smaller refund. If you believe the IRS made a mistake:

1. Gather documentation supporting your original calculation
2. Write a response letter referencing the notice number and tax year
3. Mail it (certified mail) to the address on the notice within **60 days**

If the IRS disallowed a credit, they must explain why. Look for a specific explanation in the notice — that tells you exactly what documentation you'd need to dispute it.

## If You Now Owe a Balance

Sometimes a CP12 results in a smaller refund that becomes a small balance due. The notice will show this clearly. Pay by the deadline listed on the notice (usually 21 days) at IRS.gov/payments to avoid penalty and interest.

## How Long Until You Get the Refund?

If the corrected refund is larger, expect it in **4–6 weeks** from the notice date. If you filed early in tax season, it may arrive sooner. You can check the status at IRS.gov/refunds ("Where's My Refund?").

## Common Questions

**"Should I amend my return?"**
No. Do not file a Form 1040-X. The IRS already made the correction. Amending on top of a CP12 correction creates confusion and may delay your refund.

**"Will this affect next year's return?"**
The corrected numbers will appear in your IRS transcript. When preparing next year's return, use the IRS-corrected figures, not your original filing.

**"I got a CP12 but my refund looks wrong."**
If you still disagree after reviewing the explanation, you have 60 days to write to the IRS with your documentation. After 60 days, the correction is finalized.
