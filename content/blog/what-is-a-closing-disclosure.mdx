Three business days before you close on a home, your lender is required to provide you with a Closing Disclosure. Most buyers glance at the bottom line and move on. That's a mistake — this document contains every fee, rate, and term of your mortgage, and errors in it can cost you thousands of dollars.

## What Is a Closing Disclosure?

The Closing Disclosure (CD) is a standardized 5-page document created by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) that shows:

- Your final loan terms (rate, type, monthly payment)
- Every closing cost and who pays it
- The exact amount of cash you need to bring to closing
- A projection of your payments over the life of the loan

Lenders are legally required to provide it at least 3 business days before closing — so you have time to review it.

## Page 1: The Summary

The first page contains the most critical information:

### Loan Information
- **Loan Term**: How long you're borrowing (15, 20, or 30 years)
- **Purpose**: Purchase, refinance, or construction
- **Product**: Fixed-rate or adjustable (ARM)
- **Loan Type**: Conventional, FHA, VA, USDA

### Loan Terms Table
This critical box shows:
- **Loan Amount**: The principal you're borrowing
- **Interest Rate**: Your locked rate — verify it matches your Loan Estimate
- **Monthly Principal & Interest**: The base payment (before taxes and insurance)
- **Prepayment Penalty**: Whether you'll be charged for paying off early
- **Balloon Payment**: Whether a large lump sum is due at term end

**Red flag**: If the rate, amount, or any term differs from your original Loan Estimate, stop and ask your lender to explain before proceeding.

### Projected Payments
Shows your estimated monthly payment broken down:
- Principal & Interest
- Mortgage Insurance (PMI, if applicable)
- Estimated Escrow (taxes + homeowner's insurance)
- **Total Estimated Monthly Payment**

### Costs at Closing
Two key numbers at the bottom of page 1:
- **Closing Costs**: All fees (origination, third-party services, prepaid items)
- **Cash to Close**: The total you bring to the closing table

## Page 2: Closing Cost Details

This is the most detailed and scrutinized section.

### Section A: Origination Charges
Fees charged by your lender:
- **Origination fee**: Typically 0.5–1% of the loan amount
- **Points**: Prepaid interest to buy down your rate (1 point = 1% of loan)
- **Underwriting fee**
- **Application fee**

These are negotiable — compare to competing lenders.

### Section B: Services You Cannot Shop For
Required services where the lender picks the vendor:
- Appraisal fee
- Credit report
- Flood determination
- Tax monitoring fee

### Section C: Services You Can Shop For
Required services where you can choose the vendor:
- Title insurance (owner's + lender's)
- Title search
- Settlement/closing agent fee
- Attorney fee (in attorney-closing states)

**Tip**: Shop these independently — lender-referred vendors are often 20–30% more expensive.

### Section E: Taxes and Government Fees
- Recording fees (county recorder's office)
- Transfer taxes (varies by state)

### Section F: Prepaids
Money paid in advance:
- Homeowner's insurance premium (often 12–14 months)
- Property tax deposits
- Mortgage interest (from closing date to first payment)

### Section G: Initial Escrow Payment
The upfront cushion for your escrow account:
- 2–3 months of homeowner's insurance
- 2–3 months of property taxes

### Section H: Other
Any other fees — home warranty, HOA transfer fees, etc.

## Page 3: Cash to Close and Summaries

Shows the reconciliation between what you're bringing in and what's going out:

- **Closing costs you're paying**: From page 2
- **Closing costs paid by seller**: Seller concessions (negotiated credits)
- **Lender credits**: Any credits the lender is applying
- **Down payment / funds from borrower**: Your down payment
- **Loan amount**: What the bank is putting in
- **Net Cash to Close**: The final number — what you wire or bring as a cashier's check

## Pages 4–5: Loan Disclosures

These pages contain the legal disclosures about your mortgage:

- Assumption policy (whether your loan can be assumed by a buyer)
- Demand feature (whether the lender can demand full payment)
- Late payment terms (grace period, penalty amount)
- Negative amortization language (if applicable)
- Escrow account disclosures
- Total payments over the life of the loan (the true cost of financing)

## Comparing to Your Loan Estimate

You should have received a Loan Estimate (LE) when you applied. Compare it line-by-line to the CD:

| Category | Can Increase? | Cap |
|----------|--------------|-----|
| Origination charges | Yes, only if you changed loan terms | Cannot increase without justification |
| Required third-party services (lender-chosen) | Yes | Limited to 10% aggregate increase |
| Required third-party services (your choice) | Yes, if you chose someone off the approved list | None |
| Transfer taxes | Yes | 10% aggregate |
| Prepaids | Yes | No limit — these reflect actual amounts |
| Escrow | Yes | No limit |

If the CD shows increases that exceed the allowable tolerances, the lender must **cure** the difference — either crediting you or reimbursing you at closing.

## What to Do If Something Looks Wrong

1. Contact your loan officer immediately (you have 3 days)
2. Ask for a specific explanation of any fee that wasn't on the LE
3. If a number is wrong, request a corrected CD
4. You can delay closing if needed — the 3-day review period exists for this reason
