A client, landlord, or business asks you to fill out a W-9 before paying you. You've never filled one out before and you're not sure what information you're handing over. Here's everything you need to know.

## What Is a W-9?

Form W-9 (Request for Taxpayer Identification Number and Certification) is an IRS form that businesses use to collect your **taxpayer identification number (TIN)** before paying you. It is **not sent to the IRS** — it stays with the person or company that asked for it. They use it to prepare a 1099 form, which they will send to both you and the IRS at year end.

## Who Asks for a W-9?

You'll be asked for a W-9 when you're being paid as a contractor, freelancer, or vendor — typically:

- A company hiring you for freelance or contract work
- A real estate company or landlord (for rental income)
- A bank or financial institution (for prize money, certain account openings)
- A law firm (for settlement payments)
- Anyone who will pay you $600+ during the year and is required to file a 1099

**Note**: Employees fill out a W-4 (not W-9). The W-9 is for independent contractors and self-employed individuals.

## What Information Does It Collect?

The W-9 asks for:

1. **Name**: Your legal name as it appears on your tax return
2. **Business name / DBA**: If you operate under a different business name
3. **Federal tax classification**: Individual/sole proprietor, C corporation, S corporation, Partnership, Trust/estate, LLC, or Other
4. **Exemptions**: For backup withholding and FATCA reporting (most individuals leave this blank)
5. **Address**: Where your tax correspondence should go
6. **TIN**: Either your Social Security Number (SSN) or Employer Identification Number (EIN)
7. **Signature and date**: Certifying that the information is correct

## SSN or EIN — Which Should You Use?

If you're a **sole proprietor or single-member LLC**, you can use either your SSN or EIN. Using an EIN (which you can get for free from the IRS) protects your SSN from unnecessary sharing.

If you operate as a **corporation or multi-member LLC**, use your EIN.

When in doubt, use your EIN if you have one. Apply for one free at IRS.gov/ein.

## Is It Safe to Fill Out a W-9?

Sharing your SSN or EIN with a legitimate business you're working with is normal and required by law. However:

- Only provide it to companies you've verified and have a legitimate business relationship with
- Use your EIN instead of SSN where possible
- Be cautious of phishing — the IRS does not solicit W-9s by email
- The form itself is not sent to the IRS — the requester keeps it to prepare 1099s

## What Happens If You Don't Fill One Out?

If you refuse to provide a W-9 to a legitimate requester, they are required by law to apply **backup withholding** — withholding 24% of your payments and sending that amount directly to the IRS. This creates a significant reduction in what you receive, and you'd have to reconcile it at tax time.

It's almost always better to fill out the form.

## Completing the Form

**Line 1**: Your full legal name (as on your 1040)
**Line 2**: Business/DBA name (if applicable)
**Line 3**: Check the box that matches your tax status — most freelancers check "Individual/sole proprietor"
**Line 5-6**: Address
**Part I**: Enter your SSN (boxes for 9 digits) or EIN (xx-xxxxxxx format)
**Part II**: Sign and date, certifying your TIN is correct and you're not subject to backup withholding

Don't overthink it — for most freelancers, it's Name + SSN + check "Individual" + signature.
