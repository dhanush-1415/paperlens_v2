Most people spend more time reading a restaurant menu than they do their lease agreement. Then they're surprised when they get charged for things they didn't realize they agreed to. Here's what you need to check before you sign.

## Before You Read: Know Your Rights

Lease terms can only restrict what the law allows. No lease can:
- Waive your right to a habitable unit
- Waive your landlord's duty to make essential repairs
- Include illegal discrimination based on protected characteristics
- Prohibit you from reporting code violations to the city
- Charge security deposits above the state maximum

If any clause in your lease attempts to do these things, it's unenforceable — even if you signed it.

## Section 1: The Basics

**Names**: Confirm every adult tenant is named. Unnamed occupants may have no legal standing if disputes arise. If your partner or roommate isn't on the lease, their tenancy rights are fragile.

**Address**: Verify the correct unit number, especially in apartment complexes.

**Lease Term**: Start and end dates. Is this a fixed-term lease (most common — typically 12 months) or month-to-month? Fixed-term leases offer stability but limit flexibility.

**Rent Amount**: The exact monthly amount. Is it the same every month, or does it escalate mid-lease?

**Rent Due Date and Grace Period**: The 1st is standard. The grace period (typically 3–5 days) is how long before a late fee applies.

## Section 2: Money Clauses to Scrutinize

**Security Deposit**:
- Maximum amounts are capped by state law (commonly 1–3 months' rent)
- Confirm the exact amount
- Note the deadline for return (typically 14–30 days after move-out)
- Check what conditions the landlord can deduct for — "normal wear and tear" cannot legally be charged

**Late Fees**:
- The amount per day or per occurrence
- Some states cap late fees (e.g., California caps at 5–8% of monthly rent)
- Whether there's a grace period before fees begin

**Pet Deposit / Pet Rent**:
- Non-refundable pet fee vs. refundable pet deposit — know which it is
- Monthly pet rent (ongoing charge)

**Utilities**:
- Which utilities are included in rent (water, trash, gas, electricity)?
- Which are tenant-paid?
- Are utilities sub-metered (each unit) or master-metered (split among tenants)?

## Section 3: Repair and Maintenance

This is where many landlords write clauses that exceed what the law allows. Look for:

**Repair Responsibility Split**:
- Landlords are legally responsible for: structural repairs, plumbing, heating/cooling, electrical, and maintaining habitability
- Tenants are typically responsible for: lightbulbs, minor cleaning, damage they cause

**Repair Request Process**: How do you notify the landlord of repairs? In writing? By phone? Document the process.

**Response Timeline**: How many days does the landlord have to respond? In most states, emergency repairs (heat, water) must be addressed within 24–72 hours.

**Retaliation Clause**: Many leases prohibit the landlord from retaliating against you for requesting repairs or reporting code violations. This is also protected by state law in most states.

## Section 4: Restrictions and Rules

**Guest Policy**: Can you have overnight guests? For how long? Lease clauses that define guests who stay more than X days as unauthorized occupants are common and can lead to disputes.

**Subletting/Assignment**: Can you sublet if you need to leave early? If yes, does it require landlord approval?

**Alterations**: Can you paint, hang things, or install shelves? Limits on what you can do to the unit.

**Pets**: Even if you don't have pets now — understand the policy and whether there's a fee structure.

**Smoking/Cannabis**: Whether it's prohibited inside, outside, or on the property.

**Noise/Quiet Hours**: Specific quiet hours (often 10 PM–8 AM) and noise restrictions.

**Parking**: Designated spots, guest parking rules, and fees.

## Section 5: Lease Termination

**Early Termination**: What happens if you need to leave before the lease ends?
- Notice required (typically 30–60 days)
- Early termination fee (often 1–2 months' rent)
- Whether you're responsible for rent until a new tenant is found

**Renewal Terms**: Does the lease auto-renew? What's the notice period to opt out?

**Month-to-Month Conversion**: After the fixed term, does it automatically become month-to-month? At what notice can the landlord end a month-to-month tenancy?

## Section 6: Entry and Privacy

Landlords must give notice before entering your unit in non-emergencies. The standard is **24 hours written notice** — your lease may specify more.

If your lease says the landlord can enter without notice for any non-emergency reason, that clause may be unenforceable under your state's law — even if you signed.

## Section 7: Move-Out and Deposit Return

**Move-Out Inspection**: Does the lease allow or require a move-out walkthrough? If yes, request one.

**Cleaning Requirements**: What condition must you leave the unit in? "Professionally cleaned" vs. "reasonably clean" matters.

**Itemized Deduction Requirement**: Most states require landlords to provide an itemized list of deductions within the deposit return deadline. If they don't, they may lose the right to deduct anything.

## What's Negotiable?

Even in a competitive rental market, the following are often negotiable:
- Late fee amount and grace period
- Pet fees/deposits
- Early termination terms
- Renewal notice period
- Specific repair responsibility items
- Parking fees

Get any agreed changes in a signed addendum — verbal agreements aren't enforceable.
